Ch. 1 / What we do

Unlocking the last mile.

When people have the right tools and training, local talent builds lasting wealth.

Dandelion Foundation works across education, health and economic self-reliance for girls and young women in Tanzania. We invest in people and in local, regenerative infrastructure, designed to become self-sufficient. Our first community-led pilots are being developed in Tanga.

Read the whole story without scrolling through the scenes

Girls and young women in Tanzania miss school for reasons that can be fixed: periods, pregnancy and a new baby, and the cost of school supplies. Dandelion starts there, with reliable, affordable essentials close to home. Then we invest in capability: tools, training and engineering support for local partners. We are building a shared utility to buy, make and deliver those essentials, so that the work itself becomes local jobs and local ownership. Global partners are matched to local priorities, and every collaboration starts small: one task, one scope, one review.

Immediate needs first

Keep girls in the classroom.

Periods, pregnancy and the cost of supplies keep girls and young mothers out of school. The first step is reliable menstrual, infant and school essentials, close to home and affordable.

We start by listening to women, educators and local providers, and build on what already works.

Invest in capability

Tools, training, support.

We equip local partners with tools, training and engineering support, to turn untapped potential into lasting economic self-reliance.

What we contribute is agreed around a local host’s priorities: resource mapping, coordination, documentation and follow-up.

Build compounding prosperity

Make it here. Own it here.

We are building a shared utility to buy, make and deliver these essentials, aiming to sell them for less than the shops and to create skilled, dignified local jobs.

From a useful service to a stronger local industry, the direction depends on local ownership and a viable opportunity.

Long-term horizon

From immediate relief to local ownership.

Meeting urgent health and education needs is only the starting point. Lasting change needs local productive capacity: community-led systems that pay their own way, toward shared United Nations Sustainable Development Goals.

  • SDG 4

    Quality Education

    Removing everyday barriers to school attendance, with reliable menstrual, infant and school supplies, so girls and young mothers stay in class and graduate.

  • SDG 5

    Gender Equality

    Equipping young women with the health security, practical tools and economic pathways to lead in their households and local economies.

  • SDG 8

    Decent Work and Economic Growth

    Building local productive capacity: dignified, skill-building jobs in buying, making and community distribution, in place of temporary aid.

  • SDG 3

    Good Health and Well-being

    Affordable last-mile access to essential hygiene, maternal and infant health products.

  • SDG 12

    Responsible Consumption and Production

    Regenerative infrastructure and circular supply chains, designed to run efficiently and scale to local self-sufficiency.

These are the goals the work is designed to advance. They describe aims, not results.

Deliver high-output partnerships

Global partners, local priorities.

Buying in volume and distributing through local sellers on consignment, we match global partners to local priorities, so that the benefits are shared and the work can pay its own way.

Every collaboration starts small. Agree the task. Define responsibilities. Review what happens. Then decide the next step.

The terms

One task. One scope. One review.

A first collaboration is small on purpose. It has one task, an agreed scope, and a review before anyone commits to more.

  1. 01

    Identify fit

    Choose one useful task.

    Share a priority, an existing resource, or an introduction. Keep the first request small.

  2. 02

    Agree the scope

    Decide who does what.

    Confirm capacity, costs, permissions, responsibilities, and what a useful result would be.

  3. 03

    Review together

    Deliver, learn, then decide.

    Complete the agreed task and review the result before committing to more.

Scope and acknowledgment. Each collaboration is defined by its agreed scope. Public acknowledgment requires each organization’s permission.

Ch. 3 / Our approach

Good work starts with clear principles.

  • Local voice, real authority.

    Local women and delivery partners should help set priorities and decide what is feasible. Participation must be voluntary.

  • Build on existing expertise.

    Learn from established approaches and use qualified providers. We will not create new services where a useful connection will do.

  • Safety before speed.

    No participant-facing work without appropriate permissions, supervision, safeguarding, and referral arrangements.

  • Evidence before expansion.

    Track useful next steps and full costs, including donated time. Share limitations and grow only when the model warrants it.

Ch. 4 / Questions

Questions, answered plainly.

What is Dandelion’s focus in Tanzania?

Our focus in Tanga is project development around locally defined health and learning needs, with a longer-term interest in economic opportunity. Specific activities, participant access, and delivery arrangements are agreed with the relevant local host before enrollment or delivery.

How can I support the work?

Introductions, relevant resources, technical expertise, and carefully scoped in-kind support can all be useful starting points. This website does not accept donations or provide charitable tax receipts.

How is this different from a supply giveaway?

Immediate needs matter. Our approach is to connect useful support with learning and, over time, routes to income. Any supplies would respond to a verified need and include a realistic plan for access and replenishment.

Is the English tutoring business part of Dandelion?

No. A separate tutoring business is under consideration, with separate accounts, operations, and responsibilities. Any future contribution would depend on actual surplus and an appropriate written arrangement. Participation would never require a purchase.

How are priorities and responsibilities agreed?

Local priorities, capacity, costs, permissions, responsibilities, and review arrangements should be defined jointly before work begins. Each collaboration needs a clearly scoped task and a shared understanding of what a useful result would be.

Can we use your materials or collaborate on research?

We welcome a clearly scoped discussion about materials or research. This does not provide access to participant data; any research requires appropriate permissions, review, consent, safeguarding, and qualified local involvement.
