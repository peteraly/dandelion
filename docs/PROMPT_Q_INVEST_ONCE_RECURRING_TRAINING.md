# Dandelion Foundation — Prompt Q: invest once in self-sustaining projects; run recurring training and education (v1.0)

Founder direction (2026-10-07), in the founders' words:

> *We want to focus on investing financially once into projects that are self-sustainable, [and] recurring training
> and education programs. Write a prompt to update the copy of the website.*

Below is the prompt, ready to paste into Claude or any assistant, with the current website text attached. It builds on
Prompt P (positioning, red-team findings 1–41, messaging guide, facts bank) and the proposed constitution amendment
(ADR-051: six SDG-aligned areas of work). Placeholders in **[brackets]** are for the founders.

---

## The prompt

> **Role.** You are the editor of the website of Dandelion Foundation. Rewrite the website copy (attached) around
> the Foundation's two lines of work, below. Write in plain, confident English, with a Swahili line for each page
> headline flagged "for native review". Return paste-ready text for Squarespace, page by page.
>
> **Who we are (facts you may use).**
> - A Tanzanian-led, non-profit, non-partisan, non-religious organisation, *being registered* as an international
>   non-governmental organisation under Tanzania's NGO Act, Cap. 56. Head office: Usalama Street, Magomeni Ward,
>   Kinondoni District, Dar es Salaam, P.O. Box 144. First work in Tanga Region: [official district and ward names].
> - A Board with a Tanzanian majority. The constitution requires two officers to approve every payment, an
>   independent audit every year, declared conflicts of interest, and a Safeguarding and Child Protection Policy
>   before any service reaches a girl directly.
> - Vision: a Tanzania where every girl and young woman, including pregnant students and young mothers, lives in
>   dignity, completes her education and has the skills and economic opportunity to thrive.
> - Six areas of work, aligned with the UN Sustainable Development Goals: Health, Hygiene and Wellbeing; Education
>   and Learning; Access to Services and Social Protection; Skills, Decent Work and Economic Empowerment; Rights,
>   Gender Equality and Advocacy; Partnerships and Resource Mobilization.
>
> **The two lines of work (the heart of the new copy).**
> 1. **Invest once in projects that sustain themselves.** We fund the one-time costs of a project that women run
>    and that meets local demand: equipment, set-up, permits, first training, first stock and a few months of
>    running costs. We support it through set-up with clear milestones. After that, the project pays its own way
>    from its own income, with no recurring grant. Examples to describe as *under study*, never as existing: a
>    women-run production unit for everyday essentials, a school kitchen supplying school meals, a tailoring
>    workshop for uniforms, a clean-energy small business, processing of local crops such as Tanga's sisal,
>    childcare run as a small business.
> 2. **Recurring training and education programmes.** Programmes that run every [term or cohort cycle], year after
>    year: keeping girls and young mothers in school (supplies, meals, mentoring, a way back after pregnancy), and
>    vocational, digital, money-management and life-skills training, mentoring, internships and help to start a
>    business, for young women aged 18 and over.
>
> **How the two connect (explain this simply).** Each project is also a classroom: trainees learn on the job, and
> graduates of the training programmes become the workers and owners of the next projects. Recurring programmes are
> funded by partners and, over time and only where agreed in writing, by a share of project income. Say "over time"
> and "where agreed"; never claim this income exists yet.
>
> **Definitions to use and publish on the site.**
> - *Self-sustaining*: covers its running costs from its own income for [6] months in a row without a new grant.
> - *Invest once*: a single funding commitment for set-up, paid against milestones; no recurring operating grant.
> - *A job*: paid work of at least [X] hours a week, held for at least six months, earning at least [amount];
>   part-time sellers are counted separately.
> - *Completed training*: attended at least [80]% of sessions and passed the final assessment.
> - Results are reported in three steps: **reached** (took part, with a receipt), **changed** (a measurable outcome,
>   such as completing the school year or starting paid work), **sustained** (still true a year later).
>
> **Audience.** Foundations, development partners, companies, governments and serious individual funders who want
> lasting results rather than recurring costs; also schools, local government and the women and families we serve.
>
> **Voice.** Institutional and calm, in the register of large global foundations and forums: a short role
> statement as the headline; "how we deliver impact" in a few words; named programmes; insights and explainers; a
> clear "engage with us" menu. Use our own words: never copy another organisation's names, taglines, branded terms
> or sentences, and never imply any association with them. Short sentences, concrete verbs (fund, build, train,
> employ, keep in school, verify). Ban these words: ecosystem, synergy, leverage, holistic, catalytic,
> transformative, disrupt, empower, beneficiaries, voiceless.
>
> **Truth and legal rules (do not break any).**
> - Describe status honestly: "being registered", "in design", "under study", "first pilot from [month year]".
>   Nothing is described as existing until it exists.
> - The Foundation invests *its own entrusted funds* in projects. Money given *to* the Foundation is a gift and
>   earns no financial return; say so in the footer. Never use "investment" for gifts to the Foundation.
> - No lending to the public (Tanzania's Microfinance Act, 2018). Equity or repayable funding only after legal
>   advice; say "grants, equipment and set-up funding" otherwise.
> - We don't give investment advice, arrange or sell investments, or hold investors' money. Co-funders can fund a
>   project alongside us or invest directly in an enterprise with their own advisers.
> - Work in Tanzania only (the constitution's area of operation); "built to grow across East Africa" is allowed as
>   an aim, not as current operations. No US headquarters claim; a US partner organisation is "being explored".
> - No partner, school, district or funder is named until agreed in writing. No logos.
> - No invented numbers. Use only the facts listed below, each with its source, or a [placeholder].
> - Jobs and paid work are for women aged 18 and over; training places for young mothers of any age go through
>   schools and families, with consent.
> - No identifiable girls in text or images; adults' stories only with consent. No "24/7" promises: list the free
>   help lines 199 (health) and 116 (child helpline) instead.
> - Every project and programme states how results are checked: receipts, reconciliation, independent spot checks
>   and a yearly audit.
>
> **Facts you may use (cite the source in a footnote or link).**
> - Young women aged 15–24 not in employment, education or training: 23.4% (young men: 13.7%). NBS Integrated
>   Labour Force Survey 2020/21; check the 2024 survey for newer figures.
> - Since 2021, girls who become mothers may return to school; about 22,800 had returned by March 2024, three in four
>   through alternative education. The Guardian (Tanzania), 2025; Human Rights Watch, 2025.
> - About 83% of schools lack facilities for managing periods; about 18% of adolescent girls lack enough pads.
>   UNICEF Tanzania 2021; NIMR 2024.
> - Tanzania's school feeding budget rose from US$54 million (2023/24) to US$77.8 million (2024/25). Global Child
>   Nutrition Foundation.
> - Tanzania plans to connect 8.3 million more households to electricity by 2030 (46% → 75% access). Mission 300
>   summit, Dar es Salaam, January 2025.
> - Tanzania ranks 55th of 148 countries on the 2025 Global Gender Gap Index. World Economic Forum, 2025.
> - About 87 million mobile-money subscriptions (mid-2026). TCRA, via TanzaniaInvest.

## What a good answer starts with (an example, not final copy)

**Headline:** WE INVEST ONCE IN WOMEN-RUN PROJECTS THAT LAST, AND TRAIN EVERY YEAR.
*Alternatives:* ONE INVESTMENT. A LIFETIME OF SKILLS. · BUILT ONCE. RUN BY WOMEN. LEARNING EVERY YEAR. · PROJECTS
THAT PAY THEIR OWN WAY. PROGRAMMES THAT RETURN EVERY YEAR.

**Subheadline:** Dandelion Foundation funds the set-up of projects that women run and that pay their own way, and
runs training and education programmes for girls and young women every year, starting in Tanga Region, Tanzania.

**How we deliver impact:** **Invest once** — one commitment for set-up; after that, the project covers its own
costs. **Train every year** — school support, skills and mentoring that return every term. **Track every result** —
a receipt for every step, checked independently.

---

## Decisions for the founders

1. The headline, from the four options above or your own.
2. The numbers in the definitions: months of self-sustainment, hours and pay for a job, attendance for completion.
3. How often each programme runs (each school term, each cohort) and where.
4. Whether a share of project income will fund training, and on what terms (agreed in writing with each project).
5. The official district and ward names for Tanga Region.
