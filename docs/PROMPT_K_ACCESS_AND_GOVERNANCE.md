# Dandelion — Build Prompt K: who is in, who can see what, and how the rules hold (v1.0)

Founder direction (2026-10-01), in the founders' words:

- *Think about how we can maintain control over the ecosystem and the
  system working the way we designed it: whether only participants we add
  can use the tool, or whether we should allow some others to see the info
  live but they must submit requests to be added, like customers or others.
  To keep incentives and a sustainable economy going.*

As in Prompts B–J: the response first (Part 1), the build prompt after
(Part 2), then the decisions (Part 3).

---

## Part 1 — Response

### 1.1 The short answer: closed to act, open to see, with a front door

- **Closed to act.** Only people Dandelion has checked can do anything that
  moves stock or money: sell, buy for resale, carry, hold stock, or be paid.
  That protects payments, custody, safeguarding and quality, so keep it
  closed.
- **Open to see.** Anyone can see how the system is doing, live, but only in
  totals that identify nobody. Named partners and funders can see more,
  read-only, after approval. Openness earns the trust of funders, partners,
  officials and the public, and it costs little if no person can be
  identified.
- **A front door.** Anyone can *ask* to join, in one place. Nobody gets in
  by asking alone. Two admins let in anyone who will handle girls, money or
  stock.
- **Value keeps people in, more than locks do.** Once two people know each
  other, they can trade without the system. They stay when the system gives
  them more than it costs: stock on trust, steady supply, fair and published
  prices, protection in disputes, proof of income, training. A small fee
  (Dandelion is a non-profit) keeps the reason to go around it small.

### 1.2 What is already true, and what is missing

| Control | Today |
| --- | --- |
| Accounts | **Invite only.** An admin creates the person; they confirm their own phone with a code and set a PIN. |
| Suppliers and business buyers | **Two admins** activate them. A women-owned status is confirmed by the second admin. |
| Prices, sale paths, product availability, settings, donor funding, large exports | **Two admins** |
| Customers | **Only through a local seller,** with the customer's consent confirmed by a code to her phone. Delivery partners and supplier staff can't hold customers. |
| What each person sees | **Only their own work.** Admins see everything, and it is logged. |
| Public | **Weekly totals** (hand-overs and active local sellers; numbers under 10 hidden); each receipt's own verify page through its private link; the open demo (fictional data only). |
| **Missing** | A front door for requests to join; a **second admin for people** (local sellers, hub keepers, delivery partners are let in by one admin today); a **read-only view** for partners and funders; a **rulebook** each member accepts; a **ladder of sanctions** with appeal; **signals of leakage** for admins; a decision on **cash**. |

### 1.3 Who can do what, who can see what, how they get in

| Who | Can do | Can see | How they get in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The public** | Nothing | Live totals by district with small numbers hidden; each area's price list; the rulebook; names of verified businesses and partners who agree to be listed | No login |
| **Someone asking to join** | Ask | Whether their request is open, approved or declined | The front door (§1.4), with a code to their phone |
| **Viewer:** partner, funder, district official, education partner | Nothing that changes anything | Totals and trends for their areas or programme; the live map without names; their own organisation's orders | Request, then two admins. Access expires after 90 days unless renewed; every view is logged. |
| **Member:** local seller, hub keeper, delivery partner, supplier staff | Their own work | Their own orders, stock and earnings | Request or invite → checks → **a second admin confirms** → enrolment link, phone code, PIN → **accepts the rulebook** |
| **Business buyer:** pharmacy, women-owned business, school, NGO | Buys in bulk (a record, not a login) | Their orders and receipts, by text | Request → two admins (built) |
| **Customer:** a girl or woman | Buys through her local seller | Her receipts and verify link | **Only in person through a local seller,** with consent by code. Not through the front door (§1.4). |
| **Admin** | Everything, logged | Everything | Two-step sign-in. Two admins for anything risky. |

**Live or delayed?** Viewers see live numbers. The public can see live
numbers too, provided they stay at district level with small numbers
hidden. A live map of one village with three sellers would point at
people; a district total does not.

### 1.4 The front door: asking to join

- **One page, "Join Dandelion".** It asks which role:
  - local seller;
  - hub keeper;
  - delivery partner;
  - supplier;
  - pharmacy or business (with a women-owned yes or no);
  - school, NGO or education partner;
  - funder or partner wanting to see the numbers.
- **Little information:** name, phone (confirmed with a code, which stops
  fake and mass requests), area, and a few words on why.
- **A queue for admins:** *Requests*. An admin checks by call, visit,
  documents and references, then approves into the normal invite flow. The
  second admin confirms anyone who will handle girls, money or stock, and
  any viewer.
- **Declined or untouched requests are deleted after 30 days.** Each phone
  number is limited in how many requests it can send. Nothing is ever
  approved automatically.
- **Not for customers.** Girls and women join through a local seller, in
  person; that is the safeguarding rule. At most, an adult woman who wants
  to buy could ask to be contacted by a local seller in her ward. That would
  need her consent and be deleted after 14 days. Girls under 18 would go
  through their school or a parent, never a public form (decision §3.4).

### 1.5 Why people stay on the system (the real control)

| Group | What they would lose by going around the system |
| --- | --- |
| Local seller | Stock on trust (only on the system); a steady supply at the published price; her earnings record (proof of income, later credit); protection in disputes (codes, receipts); training through the education partners |
| Hub keeper | Steady, predictable demand; restock suggestions; protection from blame for shortages or damage |
| Delivery partner | Pay known before accepting; bundled routes; payment confirmed before pickup |
| Supplier | Regular orders; payment before release; a demand forecast |
| Business buyer | Genuine, sealed products with receipts; a fixed price |
| Funder or partner | Proof per unit, which they get nowhere else |
| Customer | A fixed, published price; privacy; a receipt; a hand-over code that keeps her product hers; a genuine product |

Rules that make this work:
- **Published prices,** so nobody can be overcharged quietly.
- **Receipts and seal checks only through the system,** so "genuine" means
  "bought through Dandelion".
- **A small, visible fee,** so going around the system saves little.

### 1.6 The rulebook, sanctions and signals

**The rulebook** is short, in Swahili and English, and every member accepts
it in the app when they join. The app records which version; when it
changes, members are told and accept again. It covers:
- prices are the published ones;
- no selling Dandelion stock outside the system;
- customers only through local sellers;
- the safeguarding code;
- data stays in the system;
- disputes go to two admins.

**Sanctions,** in steps, with appeal:
1. A word (recorded).
2. A written warning.
3. Suspension from new stock or hand-overs. Any stock held is returned or
   collected, on the custody record.
4. Removal.

Two admins decide anything beyond a warning, and the person can appeal to
the founders. A **safeguarding report means immediate suspension from
hand-overs** until the safeguarding leads close it (Prompt J §4.2).

**Signals for admins, never automatic punishment:**
- **Sell-through:** stock a local seller received against hand-overs
  recorded. A large gap means selling off the system, hoarding or loss.
  Visit first.
- Hub stock falling without transfers (loss).
- Repeated hand-overs started but never confirmed.
- Price complaints.
- Payment to a number that isn't the registered payee (already checked).
- Many customers enrolled from one phone in minutes (already rate-limited).

### 1.7 Cash: the leak to plan for

Many rural buyers pay cash. The app only records mobile money. When a
customer offers cash, a local seller can refuse the sale or sell outside
the system. Either breaks the design: girls go unserved, or sales vanish
from the record and from the proof.

**Option: record a cash sale with the customer's hand-over code.**
- It proves the hand-over, not the money.
- The seller still pays her hub by mobile money, so every shilling from the
  hub upwards stays provable.
- Each seller has a limit on cash sales, and cash sales show up in the
  sell-through signal.

Founders' decision (§3.5).

### 1.8 Privacy guardrails for openness

- **The public sees totals only:** district level, numbers under 10 hidden
  (already the rule for the weekly figures), no names of people. Businesses
  and partners are named only with their consent.
- **Viewers see no personal data:** area-level only, logged, and access
  expires.
- **Requests hold little** and are deleted when not needed.
- **No form for under-18s.** Children's data needs particular care under
  the Personal Data Protection Act 2022; counsel to confirm.

## Part 2 — The prompt: what to build, in order (after Part 3)

### K1 — A public "how it's going" page

- **Live totals by district:**
  - hand-overs this week and month;
  - girl-months covered;
  - days out of stock;
  - price paid against the list price;
  - deliveries on time.
- **Also:** each area's price list, the rulebook, and the businesses and
  partners who agreed to be listed.
- **Rules:** numbers under 10 hidden; nothing below district level.
- **Builds on:** `lib/services/public-stats.ts`.
- **Tests:** small numbers hidden; no name or phone ever in the output.

### K2 — The front door

- **The page:** "Join Dandelion", with a code to the requester's phone and
  limits per phone number.
- **The queue:** *Requests* on the admin side. Approving starts the normal
  invite (K3 for people, the existing two-admin activation for
  organisations).
- **Clean-up:** declined or untouched requests are deleted after 30 days.
- **Tests:**
  - an unconfirmed phone cannot submit;
  - nothing is approved automatically;
  - old requests are deleted;
  - customers are not offered as a role.

### K3 — A second admin for people

- **Who:** local sellers, hub keepers, delivery partners and supplier staff
  become active only when a second admin confirms them. A new approval type,
  *person activation*.
- **What is confirmed:** the checks done, training, the signed rulebook, and
  for local sellers: a woman, and the safeguarding code signed.
- **Tests:**
  - one admin alone cannot activate;
  - self-approval is refused;
  - the checks are recorded with the approval.

### K4 — Viewers

- A read-only sign-in for partners, funders, officials and education
  partners, limited to their areas or programme.
- **No personal data.** Access expires after 90 days; every view is logged.
- **Tests:**
  - a viewer cannot reach any action;
  - nothing a viewer sees contains a name or phone of a person;
  - access ends on expiry.

### K5 — The rulebook

- A versioned agreement, accepted in the app at enrolment and again after
  every change.
- The home screen shows when a new version is waiting.
- Acceptances are recorded per person and version (alongside the training
  records the app keeps).

### K6 — Sanctions

- The steps of §1.6 as statuses with reasons: two admins beyond a warning,
  and an appeal.
- **The person sees their own history.**
- **Safeguarding:** a report suspends the person from hand-overs at once.

### K7 — Signals

- Sell-through, unexplained stock loss and repeated unconfirmed hand-overs
  appear on the admin home under **Needs you now**, as "visit" prompts.
- Never an automatic penalty.

### K8 — Cash sales (only if §3.5 is yes)

- A cash sale is recorded with the customer's hand-over code, with a limit
  per seller.
- Cash sales count in sell-through and are marked as cash in every report.

## Part 3 — Founders' decisions

| # | Decision | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3.1 | **The model** | Closed to act, open to see, with a front door (§1.1). |
| 3.2 | **Who approves each role** | Two admins for everyone who handles girls, money or stock (local sellers, hub keepers, delivery partners, supplier staff, business buyers) and for viewers. One admin can decline. |
| 3.3 | **What the public sees** | Live totals at district level, numbers under 10 hidden; price lists; the rulebook; consenting business and partner names. Never people. |
| 3.4 | **Customers asking to buy** | Adult women may ask a local seller in their ward to contact them (consent, deleted after 14 days). Girls under 18 only through school or a parent. Or: no customer requests at all. |
| 3.5 | **Cash** | Allow a cash sale recorded with the customer's hand-over code, within a limit per seller. Otherwise cash sales will leave the system. |
| 3.6 | **Rulebook and sanctions** | Founders write the rulebook (a draft can be prepared from Prompts H–K); sanctions as §1.6; appeals to the founders. |
| 3.7 | **Viewer access** | Who qualifies (funders, partners, district officials, education partners); 90 days, renewable. |
