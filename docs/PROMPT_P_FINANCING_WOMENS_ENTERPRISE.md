## Part 1 — Response

### 1.1 The position in one sentence

> **Dandelion Foundation turns one-time investments into women-run enterprises that create jobs and pay their own
> way, and keeps girls in school so the next generation is ready to lead them, starting in Tanga, Tanzania, with
> every shilling tracked from funder to result.**

Our role is **the trusted bridge**: we research what a community needs and can sustain, prepare and fund the
enterprise, build it with local partners, verify every result, and hand it over once it stands on its own.

**Two engines, one loop.** Women's enterprises supply what our partner schools need (meals, uniforms, hygiene
essentials). Schools keep girls learning. Girls and young mothers train and become the next workers and owners. The
Foundation's own programmes are the first steady buyer, so each enterprise has customers from the start.

### 1.2 What large funders look for, and how we show it

Funders with billions do not hand a new organisation billions. They fund a small, well-structured pilot, then follow
the evidence. The site must make that first yes easy and the second one obvious.

| They look for | How we show it |
| --- | --- |
| Local leadership and accountability | A Board with a Tanzanian majority; a published constitution; NGO registration (once issued) |
| Financial controls | Two officers approve every payment; mobile money and bank only, never cash; independent yearly audit |
| Evidence before scale | Research before funding; pilot, measure, then replicate; results published, including what didn't work |
| Sustainability and exit | Each enterprise has a date by which it should cover its own running costs; we stay alongside it, then hand over |
| A path to scale | A written playbook per enterprise type, copied unit by unit, place by place |
| Honest capacity | We start small and grow only as fast as we can deliver well |
| Alignment | SDGs 1, 4, 5 and 8; Tanzania's Vision 2050; the African Union's Agenda 2063 |
| Risk management | Safeguarding Policy, data protection, a steady buyer for each enterprise, no single funder over half our budget |
| Proof | A receipt for every hand-over and payment, sealed in a record that cannot be quietly changed |

### 1.3 Enterprise models that fit "invest once, it keeps going" (to research before the first pilot)

Each model below is a candidate, not a commitment. Each needs a local market study and a costed plan before the
Board approves it.

| Model | Steady buyer | What a one-time investment buys | Jobs | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Community production unit** (compostable or reusable pads) | Partner schools, NGOs, district | Machines, training, first raw materials, working capital | Aakar Innovations reports about 15 women per unit, plus 15–20 sellers | Not enough demand; imported materials; TBS certification |
| **School kitchens and home-grown school meals** | Schools' feeding budgets (the national budget is rising) | Kitchen equipment, clean cookstoves, working capital | Cooks, plus women farmers who supply | Late payment by schools or parents |
| **Tailoring workshop** (school uniforms, reusable pads, bags) | Schools and families | Sewing machines, training, fabric | Tailors, apprentices | Cheap imports |
| **Women's distribution businesses** (essentials, solar lamps, clean cookstoves) | Communities, our programmes | Starter stock, storage, a phone | Many part-time sellers | Thin margins; the Dandelion app already supports this model |
| **Processing local crops** (Tanga's sisal; fruit; seaweed on the coast — to study) | Processors, buyers, exporters | Equipment, training | Processors, farmers | Prices, technical skills; a longer horizon |
| **Childcare centres run as small businesses** | Young mothers in school or training, employers | Space, equipment, training, registration | Carers | Affordability; day-care rules |

**We fund an enterprise only if:** there is local demand and a steady buyer; it can cover its running costs within
[12–24] months; it is run by women and keeps places for young mothers; it uses local skills and materials where it
can; it can be copied elsewhere; and it meets our safeguarding rules.

### 1.4 How money can come in (and what the Foundation must not do)

| Route | How it works | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Grants to the Foundation** | For a programme, an enterprise, or core costs | The simplest route; the Board approves each agreement (Article 35) |
| **Enterprise grants and asset transfers** | The Foundation buys equipment and transfers it to a women's group or cooperative once milestones are met | Decide the ownership structure with legal advice |
| **Pay for results** | Funders pay per verified result: a girl kept in school for a term, a woman employed for six months | Our receipts and ledger make this possible |
| **Recoverable grants and programme-related investments** | US foundations can fund charitable projects that may repay | Needs the US partner organisation; repayments cannot become lending to the public without a licence |
| **Co-investment in larger enterprises** | Impact investors invest directly in an enterprise company; the Foundation provides training and verification | The Foundation itself offers no financial returns |
| **Development finance institutions** | Usually large tickets to companies or governments | Position as the local implementing and verification partner, not a borrower |

**The Foundation must not:** promise financial returns (it is a non-profit, Article 7); lend to the public without a
Bank of Tanzania licence (the Microfinance Act, 2018, licenses non-deposit-taking lenders; partner with licensed
lenders, SACCOS and savings groups instead); or let any partner or funder control it (Article 35).

### 1.5 What the website must not say (yet)

- **"East Africa" as current operations.** The constitution covers Tanzania only (Article 6). Say "starting in
  Tanzania, built to grow across East Africa". Working elsewhere needs a constitution amendment approved by the
  Registrar (Article 36) or separate local entities.
- **"Headquartered in the USA".** The US presence is a liaison through a partner organisation still to be created
  (Article 4). Say "United States liaison (in formation)" until it exists.
- **"Invest" with returns.** Use "fund" or "partner" for the Foundation. Gifts are not investments.
- **"Registered".** Say "being registered" until the certificate is issued.
- **Partner names or logos** before a Board-approved agreement; **invented numbers**; **photos of identifiable girls**.

#### How it works

**Title:** HOW IT WORKS

**Intro:** We are the bridge between capital and community: between funders who want lasting results, local partners
who know what works, and the women who build the enterprises.

**The five steps** (as on Home, one paragraph each):
1. **Research.** Before any money moves, we study local demand, prices, skills, materials and buyers, with local
   partners and the women themselves. We write a costed plan with a date by which the enterprise should cover its own
   running costs.
2. **Fund.** One investment covers what the enterprise cannot pay for itself at the start: equipment, training,
   licences, first materials and a few months of running costs. Each plan is approved by our Board.
3. **Build.** Our team and local partners set up the enterprise with the women who will run it, and connect it to a
   steady buyer.
4. **Verify.** Every payment, sale and hand-over is recorded with a receipt. Funders receive a short report each
   month or quarter, matched against bank and mobile-money statements.
5. **Hand over.** When the enterprise pays its own way, the women run it. We stay in touch, learn from it, and use the
   playbook to start the next one.

**What we look for in an enterprise:** local demand and a steady buyer; running costs covered within [12–24] months;
led by women, with places for young mothers; local skills and materials where possible; can be copied elsewhere;
safe for everyone involved.

**How every shilling is tracked:** funds arrive by bank or mobile money into the Foundation's own accounts. Each
amount is tagged to the funder and purpose. Payments go out by mobile money or bank only, each approved by two
officers and each tied to a receipt. A fingerprint of our records is sealed on a public blockchain every hour, so
nobody, including us, can quietly change the history. Funders can check any receipt without seeing anyone's personal
details.

## Part 3 — Decisions for the founders

1. **Headline:** "One investment. Lasting work for women." or an alternative.
2. **Scope words:** confirm "starting in Tanzania, built to grow across East Africa" until the constitution or a new
   entity allows work elsewhere.
3. **The US partner organisation:** start it now if US foundations and large donors are a priority (needed for US
   tax receipts and for recoverable grants or programme-related investments).
4. **First models to study:** choose two or three from §1.3 for Tanga.
5. **Who owns each enterprise:** a women's group, a cooperative or a social enterprise company, with legal advice.
6. **No lending in-house:** partner with licensed lenders, SACCOS and savings groups (Microfinance Act, 2018).
7. **The Transparency page:** agree what will be published and when.
